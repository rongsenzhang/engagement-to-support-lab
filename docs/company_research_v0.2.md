# Kortext: business diagnosis for the v0.2 research prototype

Reviewed **13 November 2026**. This is a bounded review of relevant public primary sources, not access to Kortext's internal product, customer complaints, financial records or research roadmap. Source IDs resolve in the register below. Product descriptions establish what a vendor publicly describes; they do not independently verify performance or causal effects.

## What business this project should address

Kortext describes a business spanning digital learning content, study tools and student engagement analytics. Its UK company page records a 2013 founding, a 2022 acquisition of Solutionpath and the 2025 StREAM-to-Kortext-stream rebranding [S01]. Its fusion page describes a Microsoft-associated foundation connecting products, institutional data and workflows [S02]. These facts place this application in a wider education product business, rather than an isolated prediction competition.

The UK company page reports two million students, while the US page reports more than three million users [S01, S03]. The populations and updating dates are not reconciled in the inspected pages. Neither figure is a reliable basis for estimating this project's market, revenue or accessible data. No revenue, profitability, headcount or customer-contract estimate is asserted here.

**Our business interpretation:** the useful applicant contribution is an auditable way to decide which observed signals merit staff attention, under realistic workload and interpretation constraints. Whether Kortext needs our particular approach remains an unvalidated hypothesis.

This evidence supports **research on context, portability, useful explanations and operational evaluation**. It does not establish a missing proprietary feature. The reference score in v0.2 is a transparent comparison instrument; its simplicity makes assumptions inspectable, not commercially novel by itself.

## Competitive context: what is already normal in this category

This is a small purposive comparison of two directly relevant vendors, not a comprehensive market ranking. Public descriptions were reviewed without customer access or a common evaluation dataset.

| Comparator | Publicly described capabilities | Implication for our differentiation |
|---|---|---|
| Jisc learning analytics [S12] | Combined attendance, online activity and assessment indicators; configurable views, notifications, case management and institutional readiness support. | Multi-source data, human support and dashboards are established capabilities. Our value must lie in a transparent research argument and evidence. |
| Civitas Learning analytics [S13] | Institution-specific outcome models, periodic retraining, prediction factors and initiative analysis. | Explainability and local modelling are not unique inventions. Compare our methods honestly; vendor accuracy cannot be inferred from marketing pages. |
| Civitas AI governance [S14] | Institutional access boundaries, controlled actions, traceability and human oversight. | A public static prototype cannot claim equivalent governance. Document the future integration boundary before handling institutional records. |

The current Civitas analytics page uses **Institutional Impact Platform**; some indexed pages retain the earlier **Student Impact Platform** name. This report follows the page actually opened [S13]. We make no ranking of these systems' predictive quality, total cost or implementation effort.

## Research decisions and questions to take into an interview

1. **Separate prediction from action.** Historical ranking quality can be measured now. Whether outreach helps requires an intervention design, suitable consent/permissions and prospective outcomes.
2. **Measure the same workload.** Compare equal integer review lists, expose misses and non-withdrawal selections, and ask staff what review capacity is realistic. Capacity values in the demo are experimental settings, not customer requirements.
3. **Test course effects before claiming generalisation.** Assessment availability may encode course differences. The [v0.3 roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) requires course-prior controls and course-specific validation before a portability claim.
4. **Make uncertainty actionable.** A staff member should know what to check: extensions, timetable changes, off-platform study or data-feed problems. These circumstances are largely unobserved in OULAD; the demo must not infer them as facts.
5. **Treat unknowns as discovery work.** Ask how engagement is operationally defined, how different feeds arrive, how staff trust alerts, what local exceptions exist, and which validation outcomes govern release. No answers from Kortext have been invented.

The existing 2014J cohort has already been inspected. New v0.2 comparisons are retrospective and exploratory. Preserve the original outputs and protocol; a new protocol does not turn observed test outcomes into unseen evidence.
